# PURCHASES

PURPOSE
Owns the vendor purchase lifecycle from purchase order through goods receipt, bill, payment and credit. Phase 4 complete: Vendors, Purchase Orders, Goods Receipts, Bills, Expenses, Payments Made, Vendor Credits, Recurring Bills, Recurring Expenses, three-way match, RBAC/audit hardening, and the RLS/concurrency/idempotency acceptance gate (`tests/test_concurrency.py`, `tests/test_workflow_e2e.py`).

OWNS
- `models/vendor.py` — `Vendor`. The buy-side mirror of `sales.Customer`, deliberately a separate model rather than a shared "Contact" with a role flag: the two diverge on defaults (payable vs receivable account, purchase vs sales terms) and nothing needs one row to be both. No `credit_limit` (that is something we extend to a customer, not track against a supplier). `default_payable_account` is a convenience a caller may read — never something a service reaches for on its own; every Bill still carries its own explicit `payable_account`.
- `models/purchase_order.py` — `PurchaseOrder`/`PurchaseOrderLine`. Creates NO accounting and NO stock. Numbered at creation, like `sales.SalesOrder`. `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`/`RECEIVED` are derived by `services/purchase_orders.py::refresh_order_receipt_status` from `GoodsReceiptLine` rows — never a stored quantity on the line. `expected_date` has no sales-side counterpart (a vendor's promised date is a fact the three-way match reads; our own fulfilment timing is ours to choose). `CLOSED` (short-close) likewise has no mirror: we control our own fulfilment but not a supplier's.
- `models/goods_receipt.py` — `GoodsReceipt`/`GoodsReceiptLine`. The inbound mirror of `sales.DeliveryChallan`. `GoodsReceiptLine.unit_cost` is the one field the outbound mirror does not need: an issue is valued from stock already on hand, but an inbound movement has no prior basis to draw on, so the receipt must state one.
- `models/bill.py` — `Bill`/`BillLine`. Numbered at POST time, like `sales.Invoice`. Carries BOTH `bill_number` (ours, from our NumberSequence) and `vendor_bill_number` (the vendor's own reference) — a sales invoice needs only the former because we are the issuer. `source_purchase_order` is a plain FK, NOT `OneToOneField` (one PO is commonly billed across several partial bills, unlike Quote→Invoice). No `amount_paid`/`amount_due` columns — see `selectors.py`. `BillLine.expense_account` overrides the item's `purchase_account` per line, which has no InvoiceLine mirror because the same item IS legitimately coded to different expense accounts on different bills (resale vs internal use) in a way revenue coding is not.
- `models/expense.py` — `Expense`. Deliberately NOT a one-line Bill: a Bill models a demand for payment sitting in AP until settled, whereas an Expense is normally already settled when recorded, so its credit side is a cash/bank account. Merging them would mean a Bill whose `payable_account` is sometimes a bank account — exactly the overloaded field that makes an AP ageing report silently wrong. `is_billable`/`customer` record intent only; Phase 5 (Projects) owns actual re-invoicing. `Expense.project` (added in Phase 5) attributes the cost to a project for profitability — a STRING FK reference, so `purchases` never imports `projects`; the dependency is database-level only and `projects.selectors` does the reading.
- `models/payment.py` — `VendorPayment`/`VendorPaymentAllocation`. Append-only from creation, like `sales.CustomerPayment` (and `inventory.StockMovement`/`audit.AuditLog`) — not merely immutable after leaving draft. A null-bill allocation is a vendor advance, the exact mirror of unapplied customer credit, except it is an ASSET (money paid out and not yet consumed) where the sales side's is a LIABILITY.
- `models/vendor_credit.py` — `VendorCredit`/`VendorCreditLine`. Mirror of `sales.CreditNote` with the debit/credit sides swapped. `amount_applied_to_bill` is frozen at issue time, same rationale as `CreditNote.amount_applied_to_invoice`. `VendorCreditLine.return_stock` is the ONLY trigger for a stock movement, and it moves stock OUT (goods go back to the vendor) where the sales-side restock moves it IN. `VendorCreditReason.DAMAGED` has no sales mirror.
- `models/recurring.py` — `RecurringBillTemplate`/`Line`/`Run` and `RecurringExpenseTemplate`/`Run`. Both `*Run` models are append-only idempotency ledgers with a `UniqueConstraint` on `(template, occurrence_date)` — the authoritative guard against generating an occurrence twice, same design as `sales.RecurringInvoiceRun`.
- `services/` — one module per document type, plus `line_items.py` (purchase-side item validation + snapshot), `three_way_match.py`, and `recurring.py`.
- `accounting_bridge.py` — `post_purchase_journal`, the ONLY purchases-layer entry point into `accounting.services`. Purely mechanical, mirrors `sales/accounting_bridge.py`.
- `selectors.py` — received/billed quantities, bill amount paid/credited/due, outstanding and overdue bills. All derived on demand, never stored.

**GOODS RECEIPT vs BILL — the no-double-receipt rule (read this before changing either service)**

A Goods Receipt moves stock (`MovementType.RECEIPT`) and posts NO accounting journal, exactly as `DeliveryChallan` moves stock without posting one. The Inventory/AP journal belongs to the Bill. A `BillLine` then either:

- **has `source_goods_receipt_line`** — the stock ALREADY arrived. Posting does NOT receive it again. Inventory Asset is debited at the cost basis THAT RECEIPT recorded, not the price the vendor billed, so the GL inventory balance and the movement-derived valuation can never drift apart; the gap between the two posts explicitly to `price_variance_account`. This is the mirror of `sales.InvoiceLine.source_delivery_challan_line`; or
- **has none** — the bill receives the stock itself, using `Bill.warehouse` (required in this case) and its own price as the cost basis. No variance is possible because the bill IS the cost basis.

Voiding reverses only stock the BILL itself received (as `ADJUSTMENT_OUT`) — never stock that genuinely arrived on a receipt, which remains a separate physical fact.

**Deliberate deviation from GRNI.** The industrial alternative is a Goods Received Not Invoiced accrual: Dr Inventory / Cr GRNI at receipt, clearing GRNI at bill time. It was NOT chosen because GRNI requires inventing an accrual account no organization has configured (root CLAUDE.md: no implicit account is invented), and because the symmetric "physical movement now, journal at the invoicing document" rule already holds on the sales side. The trade-off, stated plainly: goods received but not yet billed are on hand physically without sitting in the Inventory Asset GL balance until the bill posts. Revisit if a phase introduces configurable system accounts.

**Double-BILLING is a separate guard from double-RECEIPT.** There is deliberately NO unique constraint on `BillLine.source_goods_receipt_line`. It looks like the natural structural guard but is wrong twice over: one receipt line of 10 may legitimately be billed 4 then 6, and a uniqueness rule would permanently burn a receipt line whose only bill was later VOIDED, leaving genuinely received goods unbillable forever. The guard is a derived quantity check (`selectors.py::get_billed_quantity_for_receipt_line`, VOID bills excluded, DRAFT bills counted) taken **under a `select_for_update()` lock on the receipt line** — without the lock it is a read-then-write race, and concurrent bills each read "0 already billed" and each insert. That race was real and is covered by `tests/test_concurrency.py::ConcurrentBillingOfOneReceiptTests`.

**THREE-WAY MATCH** (`services/three_way_match.py`) is READ-ONLY ANALYSIS. It reports discrepancies and never blocks, posts or adjusts. A matcher that silently refused to post would hide the far more common legitimate case — a genuine price increase the buyer has accepted — and approval workflow is Phase 11's concern. Nothing is stored, so there is no cached verdict to go stale. Tolerances are per-request and default to exact; there is deliberately no organization-level tolerance setting, because that number belongs to the business and this phase has no configuration surface for it. Note that "100 ordered, 60 received, 60 billed" is a CLEAN match: the three documents agree, and the order's own `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` status is what conveys incompleteness. Flagging every in-progress order would bury the real exceptions.

INVARIANTS
- Every document is refused unless it is in the organization's base currency at rate 1 (`accounting.services.currency.assert_base_currency`) — multi-currency accounting does not exist yet.
- Decimal only for money, everywhere.
- Server-side totals are authoritative — `core.money.calculate_line`/`calculate_document_totals` are the only place rounding happens; never trust client-submitted totals.
- Accounting effects only via `accounting.services`, reached only through `accounting_bridge.py::post_purchase_journal`. Goods Receipts deliberately post NO journal; Bills, Expenses, Vendor Payments and Vendor Credits each post exactly one per post-or-issue/void.
- Stock effects only via `inventory.services.movements.record_stock_movement`. `select_for_update()` queries never `.select_related()` a nullable FK — PostgreSQL rejects `FOR UPDATE` across a LEFT JOIN.
- Input tax is an ASSET (`tax_recoverable_account`), the mirror of sales' output tax being a LIABILITY. It is never capitalised into the cost of goods, whether posted as a single line to `tax_recoverable_account` or split per CGST/SGST/IGST component via `tax.services.posting.build_input_tax_lines` (Phase 7). Whether a given input tax is actually recoverable is NOT decided here or by `tax` — s.17(5) blocked-credit eligibility turns on facts (personal consumption, exempt-supply use, specific blocked categories) neither module can infer from a bill. This module and `tax` only record where the caller says the tax goes.
- Posted bills/expenses and issued vendor credits are immutable, enforced at the model layer, mirroring `accounting.JournalEntry`.
- Idempotency required for financial transitions: `receive_goods`/`post_bill`/`void_bill`/`post_expense`/`void_expense`/`issue_vendor_credit`/`void_vendor_credit` are all idempotent-by-construction (locked no-op on repeat). `record_vendor_payment` has no status to exploit for that, so it relies on the `Idempotency-Key` header instead — the same two-mechanism split as sales.
- No mutable vendor balance — payable/outstanding is always derived from Bill/Payment/VendorCredit data.
- A recurring bill template may NOT contain an inventoried item: a DRAFT bill generated on a schedule has no goods receipt behind it, so it would inflate stock every period. Enforced in `services/recurring.py`, not merely documented.
- Generated recurring bills carry no `vendor_bill_number` — inventing one would trip the duplicate-bill constraint on the very next occurrence.
- Tenant isolation mandatory — every model here is `TenantScopedModel` with a matching RLS migration (`migrations/0002_enable_rls.py`).
- No class-level tenant-scoped `.objects.all()` querysets — always build in `get_queryset()`.

RBAC NOTE
`Role.STAFF` holds every purchase permission EXCEPT `RECORD_VENDOR_PAYMENT` — the one deliberate asymmetry with the sales grants. Staff may record an incoming `CustomerPayment` (money arriving is self-evidencing: the funds are already in the account) but not an outgoing `VendorPayment`, which authorises money LEAVING the organization. Asserted by `authz/tests/test_roles.py`; widening it must be a deliberate decision.

MOVED TO CORE IN THIS PHASE
Three things `sales` owned became shared when `purchases` needed them, rather than being imported sideways between two peer modules or copied: `core/money.py` (was `sales/services/calculations.py`), `core.enums.PaymentMethod` and `core.enums.RecurringFrequency`, and `core/recurrence.py::advance_occurrence`. `sales` re-exports the enums so its own import paths still work. Also fixed here: `audit.AuditLog.changes` was a plain `JSONField` and raised `TypeError` on a `Decimal` — an audit write must never be the reason a financial mutation fails. Fixed at the field definition with `DjangoJSONEncoder` (same fix and reasoning as `core.models.IdempotencyKey.response_body`), which also cleared the same latent bug in `sales.update_customer(credit_limit=...)`.

TEST GOTCHA
`PurchasesTestsBase` and `PurchasesTransactionTestsBase` both build on `PurchasesFixtureMixin`, and the split is not cosmetic: `TestCase` SUBCLASSES `TransactionTestCase`, so `class T(PurchasesTestsBase, TransactionTestCase)` resolves to `TestCase` and silently runs inside a wrapping transaction. The recurring sweeps must NOT (a `SET LOCAL` GUC leaks across a savepoint — see `sales/CLAUDE.md`) and neither must the concurrency races (they need committed rows visible to a second connection). Inherit from `PurchasesTransactionTestsBase` for those.
